> **Goal:** Read the duel board at a glance — the zones, Life Points, the phase rail, whose turn it is, your opponent's side, and the per-move timer for live or async play.

The board is laid out so **your** side is on the bottom and **your opponent's** side is on top, mirroring a real table. Here is what each part tells you.

## Whose turn and which phase {#turn-and-phase}

The **turn ribbon** across the middle reads, for example, **⚔ YOUR turn · Main 1** or **⚔ OPPONENT'S turn · Battle**. The **phase rail** at the bottom lists the six phases — **Draw · Standby · Main 1 · Battle · Main 2 · End** — and highlights the current one.

## Life Points {#life-points}

Each player's **Life Points** show as a number with a bar beside it (both start at 8000). The bar shrinks as LP drops and turns **red** when it gets low, so you can read the score without doing math. First player to 0 loses.

## The zones {#zones}

Your field is a row of labelled zones:

- **Mon** — your Monster Zone
- **S/T** — your Spell & Trap Zone
- **GY** — your Graveyard
- **Ban** — your banished (removed-from-play) cards
- **Extra** — your Extra Deck
- **Hand** — your cards, face-up to you at the bottom

Face-down cards show as a card back. Your opponent's hand appears only as a **count and card backs**, and their face-down cards are never revealed to you.

## Your opponent's side {#opponent-strip}

On a phone, your opponent's field collapses into a compact **status strip** (their name, LP, and hand count) with a **▾ expand** toggle to see their full field when you need it. On larger screens their whole side stays visible.

## Knowing when you can act {#when-you-can-act}

The app does not mark individual cards. Instead, you know it is your move when the **turn ribbon reads "YOUR turn"** and the **action panel** appears with your legal options. If there is nothing for you to do, no panel opens and the turn stays with whoever's clock is running.

## The move timer and async play {#timer-async}

When a clock is running, a **timer** shows near the top: **Your clock** or **Opponent's clock**, counting down and turning red in the final minute. Because the per-move time can be as long as days, duels can be **asynchronous** — make a move, close the app, and come back later. Just return before **your** clock reaches zero, or you forfeit that duel.
