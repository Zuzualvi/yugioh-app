**TL;DR:** Two Attack-Position monsters with 0 ATK that battle each other are both destroyed, and a 0-ATK Attack-Position monster cannot destroy a 0-DEF Defense-Position monster by battle.

Edison uses the pre-2011 battle rule for zero-value monsters (it was overturned in the 2011 rulebook). The math around 0 is handled differently from what modern players expect.

## Two 0-ATK attackers destroy each other {#mutual-destruction}

When two **Attack-Position monsters that each have 0 ATK** battle each other, **both are destroyed** by that battle. A tie at 0 versus 0 in Attack Position is mutual destruction, not "nothing happens."

**Example —** You attack an opposing **Ojama Green** (0 ATK) with your own **Ojama Green** (0 ATK), both in Attack Position. **Both** Ojama Greens are destroyed by battle and sent to the Graveyard.

## A 0-ATK attacker cannot destroy a 0-DEF defender {#cannot-destroy-defense}

A monster with **0 ATK in Attack Position cannot destroy** a monster with **0 DEF in Defense Position** by battle. When the attacker's 0 ATK meets the defender's 0 DEF, the defending monster **survives** — the attack does not break through.

**Example —** Your 0-ATK monster in Attack Position attacks a face-up 0-DEF monster in Defense Position. No damage is dealt and the **defender is not destroyed** — it stays on the field.

## Related cards {#related-cards}

This rule is about the numbers rather than any one card, but the cleanest demonstration is a battle between two 0-ATK bodies such as **Ojama Green**. It applies to any 0-ATK attacker and any 0-DEF defender — a small but real difference from the modern battle rules that can decide a board stall.
