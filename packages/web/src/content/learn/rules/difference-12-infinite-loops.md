**TL;DR:** A voluntary action that would create an infinite loop is illegal to take, while an unavoidable (involuntary) loop is resolved by destroying the card that is its primary cause — and both are human judge-calls, not enforced by the app.

Infinite loops are one of the few Edison rules that live entirely in the judge's hands. The rule is clear; enforcing it is not something a simulator does.

> **⚠ Known table-difference —** Infinite loops are NOT enforced by the app — this matches every Edison simulator. Whether an action that would create an unbreakable loop is illegal, and how an unavoidable loop is resolved, is a **HUMAN JUDGE-CALL.** The app will let you take a loop-causing action anyway. Know this rule yourself, and call a judge rather than relying on the app.

## Voluntary loops are illegal to start {#voluntary-loops-illegal}

Any **voluntary** action — a Summon or an activation you _choose_ to make — that **would create an infinite loop is simply illegal.** You may not perform it. If the only outcome of your action is a loop with no natural end, the action is not allowed in the first place.

Classic examples:

- You **cannot** Summon **X-Head Cannon** into a **Luminous Spark** / **Pole Position** loop.
- You **cannot** target **Blue-Eyes White Dragon** with **Ring of Destruction** into an **Opticlops** / **Axe of Despair** / **Pole Position** loop.
- You **cannot** chain or activate **Pole Position** when doing so would loop with **Axe of Despair**.

In each case the loop never resolves, so the game treats _starting_ it as an illegal choice.

## Involuntary loops and the "primary cause" {#involuntary-loops}

An **involuntary** loop is one created by a game mechanic the player could not avoid — nobody _chose_ to start it. These are not illegal (they already happened); instead they are resolved through the **"primary cause"** procedure: the card that is the primary cause of the loop is **destroyed by game mechanics**, which breaks it.

**Example —** A forced draw raises **Muka Muka**'s ATK in a way that loops with **Pole Position**. Because the draw was mandatory (involuntary), the loop is broken by destroying the primary-cause card — **Pole Position** is destroyed by game mechanics, and play continues.

## Related cards {#related-cards}

The recurring pieces in Edison loop rulings are **Pole Position** and **Axe of Despair**, together with **Luminous Spark**, **X-Head Cannon**, **Opticlops**, **Ring of Destruction**, **Blue-Eyes White Dragon**, and **Muka Muka**. Because none of this is enforced by the app, treat these interactions as something to recognize and raise with a judge at the table.
