**TL;DR:** In Edison the player who goes first DRAWS in their turn-1 Draw Phase (opening to a 6-card hand) but cannot conduct a Battle Phase on turn 1.

Edison runs the 2008 rules (Master Rule 1), which differ from the modern game on the very first turn. If you learned Yu-Gi-Oh after 2020, this is the first thing that will feel wrong: the starting player draws, and they still cannot attack.

## The starting player draws for turn {#first-player-draws}

The player who goes first **draws a card during the first Draw Phase of the duel.** Because both players open with a 5-card hand, the starting player begins their first Main Phase with **6 cards in hand** (5 opening + 1 for the turn).

This is the opposite of the modern rule, where the player who goes first **skips** their first draw and stays on 5. In Edison there is no skip — going first is a genuine card-advantage decision, not just a tempo decision.

**Example —** You win the die roll and choose to go first. You draw your opening 5, then draw 1 in your Draw Phase, and enter Main Phase 1 holding **6 cards.** Your opponent will draw into a 6-card hand on their own first turn.

## No Battle Phase on turn 1 {#no-turn-1-battle}

The player who goes **first cannot conduct a Battle Phase on their first turn.** You set up your board, but you cannot declare any attacks until your second turn. (The player who goes _second_ has a normal Battle Phase on their first turn.)

**Example —** You go first, Normal Summon a beater, and set backrow. You may **not** enter the Battle Phase — the turn skips straight from Main Phase 1 toward the End Phase (there is no Main Phase 2 attack window either, since there was no Battle Phase). Your opponent, going second, _can_ attack on their first turn.

## Why this matters {#why-it-matters}

Because the starting player keeps their draw, "going first" trades one extra card and a full setup turn against the fact that you cannot pressure the opponent's life points until turn 3 of the duel (your second turn). Most Edison decks still choose to go first for the board-setup advantage — but the choice is a real one, and it is different from what modern players expect.

## Related cards {#related-cards}

No single card triggers this rule — it is baked into the turn structure — but it shapes every opening. It pairs directly with the [turn primer](/learn/rules/primer-how-a-turn-works) (phases and who chooses to go first) and with **Rule #11 (End-of-Turn Discard)**, since a 6-card opening hand can put the starting player at the 6-card hand-size limit right away.
