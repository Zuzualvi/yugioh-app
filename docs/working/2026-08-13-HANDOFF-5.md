## 3 · The single most important product fact

🔴 **A duel cannot currently end normally.** Driving the real engine, life points reached 0 and the
duel did not stop — **764 `WIN` messages relayed, zero duel-end frames.** Mechanism, code-read and
independently confirmed: `EdisonDuel.step()` sets `winner` on `MSG_WIN`
(`packages/engine/src/EdisonDuel.ts:407-410`) but only returns `status:"ended"` on
`OcgProcessResult.END` (`:219-225`), and `duelSocket.ts:587-596` broadcasts a duel end only on that
status. **The engine announces a winner and the relay never converts it into an ending.**

With the clock deleted by CEO decision, **resign is the only ending a duel can currently reach.**
Requirements **C3** and **C4** carry this. **C4 also surfaced that deck-out has never been exercised
by anything in this product** and almost certainly breaks the same way — that is reasoning, not an
observation, and is marked as such.

## 7 · Requirements that exist because something failed, and will look excessive without the story

All are in the PRD. Do not let them be optimised away as paranoid.

- **A2 — cancelability may never be the test for whether the client auto-answers.** This is the defect
  that made the shipped game unwinnable: ocgcore marks **attack-target selection** `cancelable: true`,
  the client read "cancelable" as "optional", and declaring an attack answered itself with a decline.
  Proven by controlled variation. Direct attacks work and are the only attack path the E2E suite
  covers — **the suite was green and the game was unwinnable.**
- **D5 — label fidelity, in three parts.** The confirm control must name the answer being submitted;
  the name must derive from the response itself; **and it must be legible as rendered, not merely
  present in the DOM.** The third part exists because `text-overflow: ellipsis` visually cut the
  disambiguator between two identically-named candidates **while the DOM text stayed complete, so the
  gate passed.** Found by looking at the screen, not by any check.
  🔑 **This defect family has now appeared five times across two projects**, twice found by someone
  other than the author and once by the CEO personally.
- **D7 — a surface whose narration and state disagree must fail loudly, not render.** Built as
  `assertFeedConsistency`; **it threw on its first run** and the app would not boot until the
  duel-start fixture was corrected.
- **B1–B3 —** the previous PRD required a decision to *persist* and never said when it *stops*, so
  after passing turn the player sat on a stale panel with the hand physically covered. **A requirement
  that a thing persists must name the condition under which it stops.**
