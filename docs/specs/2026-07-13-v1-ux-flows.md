## What changed from the prior brief (LOCKED DECISIONS, CEO 2026-07-13)

V1 **enforces** rules but does **NOT explain illegality**. Concretely, relative to the prior brief:

**REMOVED from V1 (moved to V2):**
- The "why is this greyed out?" plain-language reason tooltips (prior brief's B3 "ⓘ why?" chip).
- Deep-links from disabled actions into the Rules & Rulings page.
- The shared `{engine reason → human string → rules anchor}` reason-mapping layer.

**KEPT in V1 (do not design these out — they are enforcement / surfacing legal moves, not explaining illegality):**
- **(a)** Marking cards/actions that currently *have* a legal action available (the "actionable" glow/dot).
- **(b) Priority / quick-effect RESPONSE WINDOWS** — when the opponent acts and the player has a legal response (quick effect / trap / Spell Speed 2+), the UI pauses and offers it; when they have none, no prompt. Engine-driven.
- **(c)** Chain-stack visualization + step-by-step resolution playback.
- **(d)** Deck-builder legality **badges** (Forbidden / Limited / Semi) and blocking illegal decks.

Disabled/illegal actions may still be shown **dimmed** (as a *state*) — they simply do not get a tappable text explanation in V1. The moment a "no" would want a sentence attached, that sentence is a V2 feature. Design leaves a clean seam for it (see §16) but ships nothing that renders it.

**ADDED to V1 (CEO 2026-07-13) — configurable per-move timer + asynchronous play:**
- Every duel carries a **per-move timer** the **inviter** picks at challenge time: presets **5 min / 15 min / 1 hr / 12 hr / 24 hr / 48 hr** + a bounded **Custom** (floor ~1 min, hard ceiling **48 hr — no "unlimited"**). Framed to the user as *"time per move"* (small = play live; large = play over days), not as a punishment.
- The clock runs against **whoever the engine is currently awaiting** (their turn *or* a quick-effect/priority response window), counts **wall-clock time whether or not they're connected**, **resets** when the awaited player changes, and on hitting zero **auto-forfeits that player** (opponent wins; end reason = **timeout**). It subsumes the old disconnect rule: *closing the app mid-duel is safe; you only lose if your move timer runs out.*
- **Async is first-class:** duels are durable across sessions and days; a player can close the app and resume a days-old game. The invitee **sees the timer before accepting**; it's shown in the Pre-Duel Room and on the field.
- **Notifications stay LIGHT (deliberate):** no push/email in V1. The in-app substitute is a **"Your move" queue on Home** (§3) that makes it obvious on login which duels owe this player a move. Formal notifications = V2 (§18).

**Architecture-agnostic:** server-authoritative vs. WASM, and image hosting, are the CTO's call. These screens assume only that (1) the engine surfaces the set of currently-legal decisions/actions for the active player, and (2) clean, legible card images exist at multiple sizes.

## §0. Global conventions (defined once, reused everywhere)

**Card visual states** (one grammar across board, hand, builder, inspector):

| State | Meaning | Rendering (color + non-color cue) |
|---|---|---|
| **Actionable** | Card has ≥1 legal action *right now* | Normal art **+ glow ring + corner dot `•`** |
| **Idle** | On field/in hand, no legal action this instant | Normal art, no marker |
| **Dimmed** | Face-down/unavailable state, or invalid target during a selection step | ~55% opacity **+ no highlight** (valid targets *pulse*; invalid ones simply don't) |
| **Selected** | Currently picked in a multi-step action | Solid accent border **+ checkmark** |
| **Face-down** | Set / hidden | Card-back art (never the face; hidden info never rendered client-side) |

No state is conveyed by color alone — each pairs color with a shape/icon/label (colorblind-safe; ~8% of men). In V1, a Dimmed card is **not tappable-for-reason**; it is still tappable-to-**inspect** (reading a card is always allowed — see §9).

**The actionable marker** is the literal implementation of "surface legal actions; don't make the player hunt." If a card or zone can do something this instant, it is marked. If nothing on your side is marked and it's your turn, the only legal move is to advance the phase — and the phase rail says so.

**Ownership cue:** *your* elements use the primary accent + a solid underline; *opponent* elements use a secondary hue + a dashed/hatched underline. Never rely on top-vs-bottom alone (landscape/tablet can reflow).

**Tap targets ≥ 44 px; body text ≥ 16 px on mobile;** both honor the OS/browser text-size setting. Every destructive/committing action is confirmable or cancelable; nothing commits on a single accidental touch (see inspect-vs-act separation, §15).

**Responsive tiers — one layout system, three densities:**
- **Phone-portrait** (~360–430 px): your-field-first, opponent as a status strip (§15).
- **Tablet / small-laptop** (~768–1024 px): both fields, condensed.
- **Desktop** (≥1024 px): full board (§6).

Same components, same interaction grammar, reflowed — so a friend on a couch phone and a friend at a desk have the same competence.

**Neutral event language (V1 constraint).** Anywhere the app narrates what happened (priority prompts, chain captions, duel log), it states the **event**, never a *judgment* about legality: "Alex activated Bottomless Trap Hole" ✓; "You can't activate that because…" ✗ (that's V2). This keeps the copy layer honest to the no-explanation decision.

**Three loops called out explicitly (per task):**
- **Challenge/accept loop:** `Home → Challenge (pick friend/share link → pick deck → set time-per-move → send)`; the challenged friend sees an **incoming-challenge banner on their Home** (which now shows the proposed time-per-move), taps `Accept → pick deck`; both land in the **Pre-Duel Room**. Decline or timeout returns the challenger to Home with a status note.

Rules & Rulings and Settings are reachable from Home and (as a slide-in) from within the Duel Field, so a player never has to abandon a duel to check a rule or bump text size.

## §2. Login

**Purpose.** Get the ~6 known friends in with zero ceremony; keep everyone else out. This is a closed group, not a product with sign-ups — no discovery, no social graph, no password-reset maze.

**Key elements.** App wordmark; one primary path (**invite link** the founder sends → sets display name + passcode on first use) and one returning path (**display name/email + passcode**); a single "trouble getting in? ask the group" line (no self-serve reset in V1 — the founder re-issues links). No "create account" button visible to the public.

**Primary interactions.** First visit via invite link → set display name + passcode → land on Home. Returning → enter credentials → Home. Invalid → inline error, no enumeration ("that didn't match" — never "no such user").

**Desktop & mobile — same single-column card, centered.** (No meaningful layout divergence; it's one form.)

```

│                               │

│      a private duel club       │
│                               │
│   Display name [___________]  │
│   Passcode     [___________]  │
│                               │
│        [   Enter  ▸   ]        │
│                               │
│   First time? Open your        │
│   invite link to set up.       │

**States.** Default · invalid credentials (inline) · invite-link setup (adds "confirm passcode") · already-signed-in (skip to Home).

**Notes.** Minimal by design — the two things people come to do (duel, tweak a deck) are one tap past this screen, so this screen must not editorialize. Session persists so friends aren't re-authing every visit.

## §3. Home / Lobby

> ⚠️ **LINK-FIRST UPDATE (2026-07-14):** the **"who's online" roster/presence** and the **incoming/outgoing directed-challenge** items below are **REMOVED**. Home keeps the "Your move" queue (top) + "waiting on opponent" list; the first primary action becomes **"Start a duel"** (→ Create invite link), and Home shows the member's **one pending outgoing invite** (copy / share / revoke, flips to "✓ joined — Enter room" on claim). Build Home from `2026-07-14-link-first-initiation-flow.md` §2; the roster/challenge prose below is historical.

**Purpose.** The group's front door. Answers two questions in one glance: **"Where do I owe a move right now?"** and **"who's around / what do I want to do?"** — in that order. With async multi-day play, the first question is the reason most people open the app at all, so it comes first. Optimized for a tiny known group, so it feels like a group chat that starts duels — **not** a matchmaking queue.

**Key elements.**
- **"Your move" queue (new, top priority)** — the durable in-progress duels **waiting on you**, one row each: opponent, a **human time-left** readout, a tiny "where we are" hint (turn/phase or "your response"), and a **[Resume]** button. **Sorted by least time remaining first**, so a duel about to auto-forfeit sits at the top with a **⚠** warning. This is the V1 in-app substitute for notifications — its whole job is to make owed moves unmissable on login.
- **"Waiting on opponent" list** — in-progress duels where the ball is in *their* court: opponent, whose clock it's on + time left, and **[Open]** (read-only review). Lower visual weight; nothing here needs action.
- Three primary actions: **Duel a friend · Build a deck · Rules & rulings.**
- **Who's online** — the group roster with presence dots (online / in a duel / offline). Small; it's ~6 people.
- **Pending challenges** — incoming ("Alex challenges you — 15 min/move") with Accept/Decline; outgoing ("waiting for Sam…") with Cancel.
- Header: your display name/avatar → menu to **Settings** and sign-out.

**Primary interactions.** Tap **[Resume]** on a "Your move" row → drop straight into that duel's live **Duel Field** (§6) at its current state — no ceremony (async resume). Tap **[Open]** on a "Waiting on opponent" row → the same field in read-only (you can review the board while it's not your clock). Tap **Duel a friend** → Challenge flow (§4). Tap a roster name who's online → shortcut into Challenge flow pre-targeted at them. **Accept** an incoming challenge → deck pick → Pre-Duel Room. **Build a deck** → Deck Builder / My Decks. Incoming challenge also surfaces as a toast if you're mid-navigation elsewhere.

## §4. Invite / Challenge-a-friend flow

> ⚠️ **SUPERSEDED (2026-07-14 — LINK-FIRST).** This "Challenge-a-friend" screen (with the roster/presence "Who" step and Accept/Decline) is **REPLACED** by a **"Create invite link"** screen: pick a deck + a time-per-move, mint a **single-use, member-only** link, share it out-of-band; the invited member opens it and lands in the Pre-Duel Room. There is **no member picker, no presence, no Accept/Decline banner**. **Build from `2026-07-14-link-first-initiation-flow.md` §3–§4;** the text below is retained only for the deck/time-per-move picker mechanics, which carry over unchanged into the Create-invite screen.

**Purpose.** "Let's duel right now" in seconds, with no external tool (beats EDOPro's Discord dependency). Two entry modes: pick a friend who's here, or share a link to pull in someone who isn't.

**Key elements.** Step 1 — **Who:** roster with presence, or **[Copy invite link]** (drop into the group chat; link deep-links the recipient to Accept). ## §5. Pre-Duel Room

**Purpose.** The social "shuffle up" moment: confirm both players, both decks, and who goes first, then Start. Preserves the ritual without being slow.

**Key elements.** Two player cards (name, avatar, chosen deck name + legality chip, **Ready** toggle); a **match-settings line** showing the **agreed time per move** (⏱, read-only here — set by the inviter at challenge time, §4) and the format (Edison); **first-turn decision** widget (coin flip / dice — winner *chooses* to go first or second, per real YGO); **[Start duel]** (enabled only when both Ready and first turn resolved); lightweight **text chat**; **[Change deck]** and **[Leave]**.

**Primary interactions.** Each player taps **Ready** (can change deck until Ready). First-turn: one animated coin/dice roll visible to both → winner picks first/second. When both Ready + choice made → **Start** enables for both; either can start (or auto-start on a short shared confirmation). Leaving returns both to Home with a note.

**Desktop:**

```
┌──────────────────── PRE-DUEL ROOM ─────────────────────┐
│                                                        │

## §6. Duel Field — DESKTOP (full board)

**Purpose.** The core screen. At all times it must answer three questions at a glance: **Whose turn/phase? What's the score? What is the game waiting on *me* for?** Everything else is subordinate to those three.

**Edison board = simpler than modern** (Master Rule 1 era): per player = 5 Monster Zones, 5 Spell/Trap Zones, 1 Field Spell Zone, Deck, **Extra Deck (Fusion + Synchro only)**, Graveyard, Banished. No Extra Monster Zones, no Pendulum scales, no Link arrows. We exploit the extra room for breathing space and legibility, not clutter.

**Key elements.** Opponent status bar (id, LP bar+number, face-down hand count); opponent field mirrored across a center **turn/phase ribbon**; your field nearest you; your **hand tray** (fanned, readable, actionable marker on playable cards); persistent **phase rail** + **End Turn**; a **duel log** rail (collapsible) that neutrally lists events; utility (Settings slide-in, Surrender, Rules slide-in).

**Primary interactions.** Click a card → its legal actions appear (context menu on desktop; same action-sheet grammar as mobile so the model is identical). Click art / hover-pin → **Card Inspector** (§9). Advance phase via the rail or End Turn. Valid targets pulse during selection steps; invalid ones stay un-highlighted. Drag is offered as an *optional* accelerator on desktop but never required.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  OPP ● Alex    LP ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓░░ 5400          Hand 🂠🂠🂠🂠 (4)      ⚑ Surrender │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                             ┌── LOG ─────┐ │
│  [GY][Ban]  [ M ][ M ][ M ][ M ][ M ]        [Extra][Deck]  │ T3 Alex NS │ │
│  [Field]    [S/T][S/T][S/T][S/T][S/T]                       │  Cyber D…  │ │
│ ──────  ⚔  ALEX'S BATTLE PHASE   ·   ⏱ Alex 21h 40m  ─────  │ T3 you Set│ │
│  [Deck][Extra]  [S/T][S/T][S/T][S/T][S/T]         [Field]   │  Mirror F.│ │

│                                                             └────────────┘ │
│  YOU ● you     LP ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ 8000                                           │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  YOUR HAND   [card] [card] [card•] [card] [card]     ( • = playable now )  │

│  PHASE:  DP → SP → [MP1] → BP → MP2 → EP            [ End Turn ▸ ]          │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Design decisions → outcomes.**
- **Your half nearest you, opponent mirrored across the center ribbon** → matches the tabletop mental model; zero re-orientation cost.
- **Persistent phase rail**, current phase highlighted, reachable phases enabled → the most common "what now?" (which phase, how to move on) is answered permanently, and turn structure is learned passively. When it's your turn and nothing on your side is marked actionable, the only lit control is the phase rail — the UI is telling you "your move is to advance."
- **Center ribbon states whose turn + phase in words** ("Alex's Battle Phase" / "Your Main Phase 1").
- **Per-move countdown lives in the ribbon, next to "whose turn"** — because it *is* an attribute of whoever the game is waiting on. Deliberately **legible, not anxiety-inducing:** it labels *who* is on the clock ("⏱ Alex 21h 40m" / "⏱ You 43m"), uses **adaptive granularity** (days/hours when there's plenty of time → mm:ss only in the final minutes), and stays a **calm neutral color when the budget is comfortable**, escalating to a warning color + icon only when the on-clock player is in the final stretch (≈ last 10%, floored). A 48-hr async duel therefore never shows a stressful ticking clock; a 5-min blitz does tighten up at the end. When it's the **opponent's** clock it's shown muted (it's information, not a call to act); when it's **yours** and running low it gets the emphasis.
- **LP as bar + number with animated deltas** ("−1800" floats, bar drains) → the scoreboard/drama is unmissable and damage math is auditable.
- **Actionable marker** on any card with ≥1 legal action this instant → surfaces options; strategy over pixel-hunting.
- **Opponent hand = face-down count only** (hidden info is never sent to the client; server-authoritative per engine research).
- **Duel log rail** neutrally lists events (turn #, actor, action) — the reviewable spine that also feeds the Summary/Replay (§13). Neutral language only (no "why").

**States.** Your turn / opponent's turn (your controls dim to *read-only* except responses) · selection-in-progress (targets pulse) · **awaiting your response** (§7 overlay) · animation/resolution playback · **resumed-async** (arrived here from Home's "Your move" queue into a days-old game — the board is exactly as you left it and the ribbon shows how much of *your* move budget remains) · **timer-warning** (on-clock player in the final stretch → ribbon countdown escalates to the warning color + icon) · **timer-expired** (the countdown hit 0 → the awaited player auto-forfeits; field transitions to §13 with reason = timeout) · duel over (→ §13). Reduced-motion mode swaps animated LP/chain feedback for instant, still-legible updates (the countdown still updates, just without animation flourish).

## §7. Priority / Quick-effect Response Prompt + Chain Stack + Resolution Playback

**KEPT in V1** (per boundary: this is enforcement / surfacing legal responses, *not* explaining illegality). This is the highest-stakes moment for clarity, and where the enforcing engine earns its keep. The UI **slows down** here (the opposite of Master Duel's pace) without feeling sluggish.

**Engine-driven, both directions.** The engine only prompts a player when they *have* a legal decision to make:
- **Responding to the opponent:** opponent acts → if you hold a legal quick effect / trap / Spell-Speed-2+ response, the prompt appears; if you hold none, **no prompt** (engine auto-passes) — you're never asked a question you can't answer.
- **Your own priority (Edison-specific — Ignition Effect Priority is IN EFFECT this format):** after you Summon, if you have a legal Ignition Effect to activate before passing, the *same* prompt offers "activate an effect or pass priority?" This must be configured in the engine ruleset (see §17) — the UI presents it identically to a response window.

**Purpose.** Never let a player accidentally pass on a response they wanted (a classic frustration); make the *order* things resolve visible, because chain order is the single most-misunderstood rule and the thing judges correct people on in person.

**Key elements.**
1. **Priority prompt** — an unmissable but *non-blocking-of-context* bar: the board stays visible behind it. States the triggering **event** neutrally + offers **[Respond ▸]** / **[Pass]**.
2. **Response picker** — if Respond: the list of *your legal responses only* (cards + effects that can be activated now, each with the actionable marker). No greyed "why not" lines in V1.
3. **Chain stack** — vertical, Link 1 at the **bottom** (resolves last) → top resolves first; each link = mini card + owner color/hatch.
4. **Resolution playback** — resolves **top-down**, brief highlight per link + a plain **event** caption; LP/board deltas animate (or snap, in reduced-motion).

**Priority prompt (overlay on the live field):**

```
        ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │  Alex activated  Bottomless Trap Hole.         │  ← neutral event
        │  You may respond.                   ⏱ You 4h 55m │  ← your move clock is running
        │            [ Respond ▸ ]        [ Pass ]       │
        └──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Response picker (only legal responses listed):**

```
        ┌──────────────── RESPOND WITH ────────────────┐
        │  •  Solemn Judgment  (Trap, from S/T zone)     │
        │  •  Starlight Road    (Trap, from S/T zone)    │
        │                                    [ ✕ Back ]  │
        └────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Chain stack + resolution (right-docked on desktop, sheet on mobile):**

```
        ┌── CHAIN ─────────────────────┐        ── RESOLVING ──
        │ CL3  Solemn Judgment  (you) ▲ │  →  ▶ Link 3: Solemn Judgment
        │ CL2  [add response?]         │           (you) negates & destroys…
        │ CL1  Bottomless T.H.  (Alex) │      then Link 2 … then Link 1
        └───────────────────────────────┘
        [ Add to chain… ]   [ Let it resolve ▸ ]
```

**Primary interactions.** Respond → pick from legal responses → it stacks as the next chain link → prompt passes back until both pass → **resolve top-down** with per-link captions → return to field. Pass → engine resolves. On mobile the prompt is a bottom sheet with thumb-reachable **[Respond]/[Pass]**; the chain stack is a peekable panel.

**States.** Awaiting-your-response · building-chain (your turn to add / opponent's) · resolving (step highlight) · empty-response (never shown — engine auto-passes). **A response/priority window is a "move" the per-move clock runs against** — so the prompt bar shows the same countdown as the ribbon, labeled for whoever is being awaited (⏱ You 4h 55m). If *your* response window expires you auto-forfeit exactly as a turn timeout would (§6/§13); the same adaptive-granularity, calm-until-the-final-stretch styling applies so a long async window isn't a stress clock. In an async duel a player can even close the app during a response window and come back — the window (and its countdown) is still there when they return.

**Notes.** Every prompt is phrased as *the question the engine is asking* ("You may respond," "Select a target"), stating events neutrally — never raw engine state, and never a why-not sentence. This is the seam the V2 chatbot slots into with no layout change.

## §9. Card Inspector (reused everywhere)

**Key elements.** Large card art; **full name, type line, Attribute, Level/Rank (Level only in Edison), ATK/DEF**; **complete effect text at readable size**; set/passcode; for Edison, static **rulings/notes** where we have verified content (read-only reference text — *not* a live "why did this happen" — that's V2). **Purpose.** Meet the best incumbent builders (Dueling Nexus, edisonformat.net) on filtering/preview, then beat them on **live legality visualization** and **being genuinely usable on mobile** (§11 note). **Key elements.**
- **Search bar** + **Filters** (the proven Nexus set): card pool (Edison on by default, toggleable), card type (Monster/Spell/Trap), monster Type, Attribute, Level, ATK/DEF range, effect-text search, banlist status.

## §15. The Mobile 2-player Board — the central V1 UX risk (carried forward + refined)

A 2-player, ~22–24-zone board on a ~375-px phone is the single biggest UX risk in V1. Cramming both full fields at readable size is impossible — so we **don't**. Refined strategy:

**(a) Portrait "your-field-first" vertical stack — you never zoom to act.**
Your half gets the bottom ~55–60% at full size. The opponent's half is compressed into a **status strip** up top (their LP, hand count, and a *thumbnail* zone row) that **expands to full detail on tap (▾)**. Rationale: ~90% of the time you act on *your* board and only *read* the opponent's. Optimize the common case; make the rare case one tap away.

```
┌───────────────────────┐        (opp expanded via ▾)
│ Alex  LP 5400  ✋4  ▾  │        ┌───────────────────────┐
│ [m][m][m][m][m] mini   │        │ ▴ Alex  LP 5400  ✋4    │
│ [s][s][s][s][s] mini   │        │ [GY][Bn] [M][M][M][M][M]│
├───────────────────────┤        │ [Fld]    [S][S][S][S][S]│  full opp board
│  ⚔ YOUR MAIN 1  ⏱ 21h  │        │           [Deck][Extra] │  (tap ▴ to collapse)
├───────────────────────┤        └───────────────────────┘
│ [S/T][S/T][S/T][S/T][S]│  ← YOUR board, full size, ≥44px targets
│ [ M ][ M ][ M ][ M ][M]│
│ GY  Ban   Field  Deck  │
├───────────────────────┤
│  YOUR HAND  (swipe →)  │  ← horizontally scrollable fan
│  [card][card][card•]   │
├───────────────────────┤
│ ◀ DP SP [MP1] BP MP2 EP│  ← compact phase rail
│           [ End Turn ▸]│
└───────────────────────┘
```

**(b) Tap-to-select, never drag-required.** Drag is fragile on touch (scroll conflicts, fat-finger drops) and impossible one-handed. Model: **tap a card → a bottom action-sheet of its legal actions → tap the action → tap the destination (which pulses).** Every action is 2–3 forgiving, cancelable taps. In V1 the sheet lists **only currently-legal actions** (no "why not" line — that's V2). Nothing commits until the final confirm/destination tap.

```
   Tap a hand card →              (V1: legal actions only; no "why not?")
   ┌───────────────────┐
   │  Stardust Dragon  │
   │ ▸ Special Summon  │
   │ ▸ Set             │
   │ ✕ Cancel          │
   └───────────────────┘
```

**(c) Inspect/zoom is a separate, deliberate gesture.** Tap the card's **art** (not its action region) or **long-press** → full-screen Card Inspector (§9). Separating "inspect" from "act" kills the Duelingbook problem where every touch risks doing something. **Outcome:** you can always read a card without fear of triggering it.

**(d) Response prompts (§7) are bottom sheets** so **[Respond]/[Pass]** sit under the thumb; the chain stack is a peekable panel above them. The opponent's status strip stays visible so you can see what you're responding to. The sheet header carries the same **⏱ per-move countdown** as the turn ribbon (a response window is on the clock too), styled calm-until-final-stretch so a long async window doesn't read as a stress prompt.

**(e) Landscape offered, not required.** Landscape can show a scaled-down both-fields "tabletop" view for players who want it, but portrait one-field-first is primary — phones are held portrait and Edison duels run long.

**(f) Responsive continuity.** Phone-portrait (this) → tablet (both fields, condensed) → desktop (§6 full) is one component system reflowed, so the interaction grammar (tap → action sheet → pulsing destination; tap-art-to-inspect) is *identical* everywhere. One mental model; a couch-phone friend and a desk friend are equally competent.

**(g) Per-move countdown + async resume on mobile.** The countdown rides in the compact **turn/phase strip** ("⚔ YOUR MAIN 1 ⏱ 21h"), mirroring the desktop ribbon (§6) with identical adaptive-granularity, calm-until-the-final-stretch styling — muted on the opponent's clock, emphasized when it's yours and running low. Because phones are where async play actually happens (check the app on the couch, make one move, put it down), the **[Resume] path from Home's "Your move" queue (§3) lands directly here** at the live game state — the single most common mobile entry into a duel. The board looks exactly as you left it days ago; the only thing that changed is the clock, and the strip shows how much of your move budget is left.

## §16. Accessibility & readability requirements (non-negotiable — card text *is* the game)

- **Two-tier card rendering.** At board/zone size: art + name + key stats (ATK/DEF/Level/Attribute) legible; **never** require reading full effect text at zone size. Full text lives one deliberate tap away in the Inspector (§9).
- **Tap targets ≥ 44 px; body/effect text ≥ 16 px on mobile.** Type scale honors the OS/browser text-size setting and the in-app Text-size control (§14).
- **No meaning by color alone.** Banlist status (🚫/①/②), ownership (you vs. opponent), and card states (actionable/dimmed/selected/valid-target) each pair color with an icon, shape, or label. Colorblind-safe mode strengthens these cues. (~8% of men have a color-vision deficiency.)
- **Reduced-motion mode** keeps LP-swing, chain, and resolution feedback fully informative without animation — for vestibular sensitivity and low-end phones. Replays and LP deltas snap instead of animate.
- **Contrast + dark mode** shipped natively (light/dark/system), meeting WCAG AA contrast for text and essential UI.
- **The board answers the three questions at a glance** (whose turn/phase, the score, what it's waiting on you for) at every text size and in both themes — accessibility can't break the core legibility contract.
- **Focus/keyboard on desktop** (nice-to-have but low-cost): phase rail, End Turn, and prompt buttons are keyboard-reachable; the enforcing model means keyboard users can't make illegal moves either.

## §17. Handoff notes / open questions for the CTO & engineering

1. **Legal-action surfacing is the contract this UI depends on.** Every "actionable marker," action sheet, targeting pulse, and response prompt assumes the engine can tell the client *the set of currently-legal decisions/actions for the active player* (which ocgcore does via decision-request messages). V1 needs **which actions are legal**, not *why others aren't* — so the removed V2 reason-mapping layer is genuinely out of scope and does not block V1.
2. **Edison ruleset must be configured for the 2010 environment**, and the response-window UI (§7) depends on it: **Ignition Effect Priority is IN EFFECT** (turn player may activate an Ignition Effect with priority after a Summon before passing SS2 window); **player going first draws on turn 1** (pre-MR3); one Field Spell zone behavior of the era; Damage Step timing pre-MR3. Confirm the engine's ruleset flags express MR1-era priority, or flag the gap early — this shapes what prompts the field must render.
3. **Hidden information never reaches the client.** Opponent hand = count only; face-down cards = card-back + "Set card," non-inspectable (§6/§9). Assumes server-authoritative state (per engine research) — if a WASM/local path is used for goldfish/replay, it must not expose hidden zones in live 1v1.
4. **Deck legality data.** The builder badges (§10/§11) and challenge-time blocking (§4) need the frozen Edison passcode allow-list + `lflist.conf` (from the edison-pool-lock decision). Copy limits (F0/L1/S2) and bin limits (Main 40–60, Extra ≤15, Side ≤15) are enforced client-side for UX responsiveness **and** server-side for authority.
5. **Card images at multiple sizes.** Field/hand/thumbnail/inspector all need crisp, text-legible art on hi-dpi phones. Source/hosting is the CTO's call; the design assumes it's available and legible (task premise).
6. **`.ydk` import/export** (§11/§12) for interop with the group's existing tools — confirm format parity.
7. **Replay storage.** §13 assumes at least the most-recent duel's message stream is persisted; retention depth is a storage decision.
8. **The V2 seam exists but ships nothing.** §7 prompts, §9 Inspector rulings block, and §13 log are laid out so a later "why did/couldn't that happen?" affordance drops in without relayout. Do **not** build the reason-mapping layer, the disabled-action tooltips, or the disabled-action → rules deep-links in V1.
9. **Per-move timer + async durability are server-authoritative.** The countdown shown in the ribbon (§6/§15) and response bar (§7) reflects a **server-owned wall-clock deadline** on whoever the engine is awaiting; the client only renders it (and must not let a paused/backgrounded tab, sleep, or clock skew fake extra time). The deadline **resets when the awaited player changes** and **counts down whether or not either player is connected** — so expiry, and the resulting timeout auto-forfeit (§13), can be adjudicated by the server even with nobody online. This requires **durable per-duel state** that survives both players being offline for days, plus a "who is awaited + deadline timestamp" the client can read on resume. The **"Your move" queue (§3)** is a read of this same state across all of a player's in-progress duels, sorted by remaining time. See the per-duel-forfeit-timer / async decision record for the confirmed semantics.

## §18. Explicitly NOT in V1 (so nothing creeps back in)

- ❌ "Why is this greyed out?" plain-language reason tooltips on disabled cards/actions.
- ❌ Deep-links from disabled actions into the Rules & Rulings page.
- ❌ The `{engine reason → human string → rules anchor}` reason-mapping layer.
- ❌ The in-duel "why did that happen?" chatbot (V2).
- ❌ **Formal push / email / SMS notifications (V2).** V1 ships the per-move timer and async durable duels, but the *only* in-app cue that a move is owed is the **"Your move" queue on Home (§3)** — surfaced on login. Friends nudge each other out-of-band (their group chat) if a duel is about to time out. No notification service, no background delivery, no per-user notification preferences in V1.
- ❌ An **"unlimited" / no-timeout duel option** — every duel has a per-move deadline; the ceiling is 48 hr, deliberately (no way to create a never-forfeiting game).
- ❌ In-flight timer **renegotiation / pause / extension** — the time-per-move is set by the inviter, accepted by the invitee, and locked at Start; no pausing or extending mid-duel in V1.
- ❌ Ranked/matchmaking, AI opponent, spectating, tournaments, cosmetics/gacha, native app — none serve six friends dueling each other.

Disabled/illegal actions may still be shown **dimmed as a state**; they simply carry no tappable text explanation in V1. The Rules & Rulings page (§ below) still exists as a standalone reference — it just isn't wired to disabled-action deep-links in V1.
